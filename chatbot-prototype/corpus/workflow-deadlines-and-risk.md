# Workflow deadlines and revenue risk

## Deadline types

The firm tracks three kinds of deadlines: court-imposed, client-requested, and internal. Court-imposed deadlines are fixed and, if missed, expose the client to sanctions and the firm to reputational damage. Client-requested dates are negotiated but still contractual. Internal deadlines are self-imposed quality gates that protect the first two.

## Why late matters

A missed production date rarely means the data is recovered later; usually it means a partial production, an extension request, or a client escalation. Partial production creates rework in processing, because load files must be regenerated. Extensions erode margin, because processing staff are re-deployed at short notice. Escalations cost account managers time that could have been spent on new business. In revenue terms, a critical deadline breached in the final 30 days of a matter's production window typically costs more than the profit in that same window.

## High-risk markers

Matters are flagged high-risk when any of the following are true: the production date is within 30 days and processing is not complete; new custodians were added after the collection plan sign-off; the matter is running over the agreed escalation budget; or the client has already escalated once this quarter. These markers are combined into a simple risk score maintained in the matter management database.
