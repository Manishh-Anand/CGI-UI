# Client portfolio overview

## Portfolio shape

The client book is concentrated in three sectors: banking and financial services, law firms acting for large corporates, and a smaller government/regulatory line. Banking and financial services is the largest by revenue, driven by regulatory and cross-border matters. Law firm clients contribute the largest number of small matters. All names in this document are fictional stand-ins for planning purposes.

## Sector notes

Banking clients run the largest matters by data volume and the strictest deadlines, because their regulators impose fixed response windows. Legal-sector clients buy specialised help on individual matters. The government line buys forensic work and collections. This mix is why the firm's delivery capacity has two shape profiles: a few very large, long-running matters and a long tail of small ones.

## Growth signals

The fastest growing demand is for managed services on active litigation, where the client hands the firm a running matter rather than a one-off collection. Managed services matters have longer tenures, steadier staffing and higher renewal rates. The strategy note for this year is to shift the mix towards managed services while keeping the one-off collection business as a feeder.

## Flagship matters

Two matters are treated as flagship references for new business: a large banking-sector investigation (codenamed Matter Meridian) and a technology-company trade dispute. Both are win/loss-referable: delivered on time under regulatory pressure, making them the first case studies shown to prospective clients.
