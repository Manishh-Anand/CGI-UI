# Market and industry notes

## Industry direction

The eDiscovery market's centre of gravity is moving from one-off matters to continuous/monitored review: active litigation where data arrives in waves and the provider review as it lands. The same trend shows up in client RFPs as demand for "always-on" review capacity. Data volume growth, driven by chat and collaboration platforms, is the most-cited procurement driver. Figures in this document are synthetic planning assumptions, not published market data.

## AI in the workflow

Generative AI is entering review workflows in two ways: as drafting assistance (summaries, issue coding suggestions) and as TAR model acceleration. The industry debate is about defensibility: coding decisions made by a model still need an audit trail. The firm's own position is that AI accelerates review but does not replace the reviewer sign-off, which must be recorded per document.

## Pricing pressure

Pricing pressure is coming from fixed-fee competitors who bundle processing and review. The observed pattern is that fixed-fee bids win on mid-size matters but lose on very large or very complex ones, where risk is harder to price. This drives the firm's pricing strategy: fixed-fee propositions for mid-size work, time-and-materials with clear caps for the large matters.

## Regulatory context

Regulatory deadlines continue to be the strongest driver of urgent demand. Markets with the most compressed regulatory windows (banking oversight, cross-border data requests) are the firm's best-fit segments because they reward process defence and deadline discipline.
